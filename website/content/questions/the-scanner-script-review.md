Hi Martin,

Before anything else: **nothing here has been generated.** This is a script, nothing more, and it stays that way until you've told us it's fine to proceed — the same rule this series already applies to anyone else's real, documented work. If you'd rather this not exist at all, say so below and that's the end of it.

The idea: a short animated episode, in the same style as [the first one we actually made](/videos/are-you-dreaming-01-the-bell/), based on your 2012 combined EEG/fMRI case study of dream lucidity. The honest motivation is on [the donate page](/donate/) if you want the context for why this exists at all.

Below is the full shot-by-shot draft, exactly as it stands right now, followed by our own truth ledger — what we're confident is documented, what we found in secondary sources but couldn't verify against your paper's full text, and what's invented for the screen. Then a few specific questions, below that.

## Before anything else: this is not Episode 1

Episode 1 could go ahead without anyone's permission because every character in it is invented. This one is different in one load-bearing way: you're a real, named, living scientist, and the whole point of the episode is your actual published work. So this is a draft for your review, not a production plan.

The study's other real person — the dreamer — was never named in anything we found, so they're depicted as a fully anonymous, generic figure: no claimed resemblance to anyone, because there's no identity to resemble.

## How this script is built

Same method as Episode 1: one Higgsfield prompt per shot, written to the MCSLA formula (Model · Camera · Subject · Look · Action), with reusable Character Elements instead of re-describing people each time.

**One constraint unique to this episode:** the researcher character is not generated from your photo and is not art-directed toward resembling you — stylization, not likeness, by design.

- **Style block:** *"Clean modern documentary-animation style, soft linework, muted clinical blue-grey palette for the waking lab and non-lucid dream, bursting into warm saturated color the instant lucidity begins, gentle hand-drawn motion, no harsh outlines."*

## Act 1 — Into the scanner

**Shot 1 — establishing (≈5s)**
> A sleep laboratory at night: a glass-walled control room beside an MRI scanner room, monitors glowing, cables coiled, everything quiet. Camera: slow push in through the control-room glass toward the scanner. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "Most of what we know about dreaming comes from someone describing it after they woke up. A sleep lab in Munich wanted something harder: to watch one happen."

**Shot 2 — prepping (≈6s)**
> The dreamer lying on the scanner bed, an EEG cap being fitted by gloved hands, electrode wires gathered and taped, the MRI bore open behind them. Camera: static, slight handheld breathing. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "An EEG cap, to read the brain's electrical rhythm. An MRI scanner, to see which regions were active — live, at the same time. No one had done both at once for a lucid dream before."

**Shot 3 — the researcher (≈5s)**
> The researcher character, seated at the control-room monitoring station, multiple screens showing live traces, eyes moving between them. Camera: slow pan across the monitors to the researcher's face. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "And one more thing: a way for the dreamer to tell them, from inside the dream, the exact moment it became lucid."

## Act 2 — The signal

**Shot 4 — REM begins (≈5s)**
> Close on an EEG trace on a monitor: rapid eye-movement waveforms beginning, a small "REM" label. Camera: macro push into the trace. Style: [Style block], muted blue-grey.

**VO:** "REM sleep starts. The eyes move. So far, the brain looks like any other dream."

**Shot 5 — the dream, not yet lucid (≈5s)**
> The dreamer's figure inside an abstract, dim blue-grey dreamscape — present, moving, but unaware, no reaction to anything around them. Camera: slow drift past the figure. Style: [Style block], deliberately flat and muted.

**VO:** "Non-lucid: here, but not knowing it."

**Shot 6 — the agreed signal (≈6s)**
> Close on the same EEG trace, now showing a distinct, regular left-right-left-right eye-movement pattern, visually different from the earlier random REM trace, highlighted in the monitor software. Camera: static close-up. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "Then: a signal. Left, right, left, right — agreed on beforehand, while still awake. One of the only things a sleeping brain can still do on command."

**Shot 7 — recognition in the control room (≈5s)**
> The researcher leaning toward the monitor, pointing something out, a second researcher's hand entering frame to look too. Camera: push in on the researcher's face. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "On the other side of the glass, someone is watching for exactly that."

**Shot 8 — the pivot (≈6s)**
> The abstract dreamscape from Shot 5, now bursting from blue-grey into vivid, saturated color in a single bloom, centered on the dreamer's figure, who turns and reacts for the first time. Camera: slow push in, color bloom transition mid-shot. Style: [Style block], the most saturated moment of the episode.

**VO:** "And somewhere behind the forehead, regions that usually go quiet in a dream switch back on."

**Shot 9 — the brain lights up (≈6s)**
> A soft, schematic brain visualization (not literal medical imaging), frontal and parietal regions glowing warm color against a cooler background, labels fading in gently: "frontopolar cortex," "precuneus." Intercut rhythm with the now-vivid dream from Shot 8. Camera: slow orbit around the brain visualization. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "Frontal regions. The precuneus. Areas tied to self-reflection — active enough, inside the dream, to notice it's a dream."

**Shot 10 — the whole picture (≈6s)**
> Wide shot: the dreamer in the scanner, the researcher at the monitors beyond the glass, the vivid dream visualization and glowing brain regions overlaid faintly between them, as if the room itself contains both. Camera: slow crane up. Style: [Style block].

**VO:** "One sleeping brain, lucid, measured in real time. A single night, saying something centuries of descriptions after the fact couldn't: this is what it looks like while it's happening."

## Our truth ledger

**Documented:** Dresler M, Wehrle R, Spoormaker VI, Koch SP, Holsboer F, Steiger A, Obrig H, Sämann PG, Czisch M, "Neural Correlates of Dream Lucidity Obtained from Contrasting Lucid versus Non-Lucid REM Sleep: A Combined EEG/fMRI Case Study," *Sleep* 2012;35(7):1017–1020, DOI 10.5665/sleep.1974. Explicitly a case study, not a large-group study. Simultaneous EEG and fMRI during overnight sleep in the scanner. Reported finding: increased activity during lucid versus non-lucid REM in frontal and parietal regions, consistently cited as including the precuneus and frontopolar cortex — regions associated with self-reflection and metacognition. At the time of this study, your affiliation was the Max Planck Institute of Psychiatry, Munich (with co-authors also at the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences, Leipzig, and Charité Berlin) — not Donders/Radboud, where you are now, years later.

**Reported by secondary sources, not independently verified by us:** the exact number of participants (one secondary source states four participants total, with one producing two lucid-REM episodes long enough to analyze); that the left-right-left-right eye-movement signal specifically was used in this 2012 study (confirmed for your closely related 2011 *Current Biology* paper, and near-certain here, but not independently confirmed against this paper's own full text); a later review's framing that this remains the only fMRI ever performed of an actively lucid dreaming brain, which would imply no direct replication to date.

**Dramatized:** the specific control room, the specific dialogue, the specific gestures and expressions of both the researcher and the dreamer, and the "years later" office in Act 3 are all invented for the screen. The dreamer is anonymous in every source we found, so their depiction is fully generic by necessity. You are depicted deliberately as a non-likeness, per the constraint above.

**To check before production:** everything in the middle section above, directly against your paper's full text — and, obviously, your answers below.
