**An open-source dream machine you can build yourself.**

You put on a Muse headband and go to sleep. An app listens to your brainwaves. A small program on your own laptop guesses your sleep stage. When it is fairly sure you are dreaming, it plays a soft sound that you trained yourself, that evening, to link with the question *"Am I dreaming?"*. And if you realize you are, you can signal back from inside the dream with your eyes: left, right, left, right.

Will it give you lucid dreams? Honest answer: maybe, sometimes. Sound cues during REM sleep have helped some people in lab studies; at home the evidence is weaker. OpenLucid is a tool to find out, honestly, what works for you.

- **Local:** your brain data never leaves your computer.
- **Sound only:** no electricity goes into anyone's head.
- **No headband?** A replay mode lets you try everything with a recorded night.
