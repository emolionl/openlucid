type: "halloffame"
milestones:
  - year: "~350 BCE"
    title: "The first written description"
    who: "Aristotle"
    citation: "On Dreams (Περὶ ἐνυπνίων)"
    confidence: "historical"
    summary: "Wrote that \"often, when one is asleep, there is something in consciousness which declares that what then presents itself is but a dream\" — the first surviving description of knowing you're dreaming while still asleep. Philosophy, not science: nobody measured anything for another 2,300 years."
    link: "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_Dreams_(Aristotle)"
    link_label: "Read it"
  - year: "1867"
    title: "The first how-to guide"
    who: "Marie-Jean-Léon Lecoq, Marquis d'Hervey de Saint-Denys"
    citation: "Les Rêves et les Moyens de les Diriger (Paris: Amyot), published anonymously"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "The first systematic attempt to direct one's own dreams on purpose, describing dreams in which the dreamer knows they're dreaming. Used the French phrase \"rêve lucide\" — 46 years before the English term existed."
    note: "His authorship was only confirmed later, in a footnote of Alfred Maury's 1878 Le sommeil et les rêves."
    link: "https://archive.org/details/lesrvesetlesmoye00herv"
    link_label: "See the original"
  - year: "1913"
    title: "The English term arrives — sort of"
    who: "Frederik van Eeden"
    citation: "\"A Study of Dreams,\" Proceedings of the Society for Psychical Research, vol. 26"
    confidence: "contested"
    summary: "Usually credited with coining \"lucid dream\" in English."
    note: "Two catches worth knowing: his own sense of \"lucid\" meant having insight (as in a lucid interval), not today's \"I know I'm dreaming\" — and the French term had already existed since 1867 (see above). \"Introduced the English term\" holds up better than \"invented the concept.\""
  - year: "1968"
    title: "A category worth studying on its own"
    who: "Celia Green"
    citation: "Lucid Dreams (Institute of Psychophysical Research)"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "First to argue lucid dreaming deserved to be studied as its own distinct category, separate from ordinary dreaming — and proposed it was linked to REM sleep, a claim the lab would confirm a few years later. Both pioneers below name this book as where they first learned the phenomenon existed."
  - year: "1975"
    title: "The first signal sent out of a dream"
    who: "Keith Hearne & Alan Worsley"
    citation: "Hull University; documented in Hearne's 1978 PhD thesis, Lucid Dreams: An Electro-Physiological and Psychological Study"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Worsley, the research subject, moved his eyes left-right-left-right on a pre-arranged cue from inside a confirmed REM-sleep dream — the first real-time signal ever sent out of a dream and captured on a physiological recording."
    note: "A technical correction worth keeping straight: the EEG channel confirmed the sleep/REM stage, but the signal itself was recorded on the EOG (eye-movement) channel. It's also unclear whether this result was published in a peer-reviewed journal at the time or only in the unpublished thesis — treat \"first published proof\" framings of this moment with some caution."
    link: "https://www.keithhearne.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Lucid-Dreams-LQ.pdf"
    link_label: "Hearne's own PDF of the record"
  - year: "1980–81"
    title: "Independent confirmation, and the first published proof"
    who: "Stephen LaBerge, with Lynn Nagel, William Dement & Vincent Zarcone"
    citation: "\"Lucid Dreaming Verified by Volitional Communication during REM Sleep,\" Perceptual and Motor Skills, 52, 727–732 (1981)"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "LaBerge independently verified the same kind of eye-signal from inside REM sleep for his Stanford PhD, apparently without knowing of Hearne's prior work, then published it — the result most often cited as the field's founding, peer-reviewed proof."
    note: "Hearne's unpublished thesis predates this by about two years, so who \"got there first\" depends on whether a thesis counts as publication."
  - year: "1987"
    title: "The first dedicated research institute"
    who: "Stephen LaBerge"
    citation: "The Lucidity Institute"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Founded the first organization dedicated to lucid-dream research and public education, and developed the DreamLight (later the NovaDreamer) — a sleep mask that flashes LEDs during detected REM sleep. The direct ancestor of the sound-cueing approach OpenLucid itself is built around."
  - year: "2012"
    title: "Lucidity, seen live in a brain scanner"
    who: "Martin Dresler et al."
    citation: "\"Neural Correlates of Dream Lucidity Obtained from Contrasting Lucid versus Non-Lucid REM Sleep: A Combined EEG/fMRI Case Study,\" Sleep, 35(7), 1017–1020"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "The first time anyone combined EEG and fMRI at once to watch a lucid dream happen: frontal and parietal regions tied to self-reflection — including the precuneus and frontopolar cortex — switched back on the moment lucidity began."
    note: "Covered in full, shot by shot, on a dedicated page on this site."
    link: "/questions/the-scanner-script-review/"
    link_label: "See the full writeup"
  - year: "2014"
    title: "The first time anyone caused it, not just watched it"
    who: "Ursula Voss et al."
    citation: "\"Induction of self awareness in dreams through frontal low current stimulation of gamma activity,\" Nature Neuroscience, 17, 810–812"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Electrical brain stimulation in the gamma band (40Hz), applied during confirmed REM sleep, induced lucid dreaming in 77% of people tested — the first causal, not just correlational, evidence."
    note: "A small sample; striking, but still waiting on wider replication."
    link: "https://www.nature.com/articles/nn.3719"
    link_label: "Read the paper"
  - year: "2015"
    title: "A physical signature in the brain"
    who: "Elisa Filevich, Martin Dresler, Timm Brick & Simone Kühn"
    citation: "Voxel-based morphometry study (frequent vs. infrequent lucid dreamers)"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Frequent lucid dreamers turned out to have measurably more gray matter in two self-reflection-linked frontal regions, plus the anterior cingulate cortex, a motor-planning area, and the hippocampus — the first evidence the trait leaves a physical trace, not just a reported experience."
    note: "Often misattributed elsewhere to a later Baird/Mota-Rolim/Dresler paper — that 2019 paper is a review that cites this one, not the original finding."
  - year: "2018"
    title: "A second, separate brain signature"
    who: "Remington Baird, Lampros Perogamvros, Francesca Castelnovo et al."
    citation: "\"Frequent lucid dreaming associated with increased functional connectivity between frontopolar cortex and temporoparietal association areas\""
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Frequent lucid dreamers show stronger functional connections between the frontopolar cortex and regions that integrate sensory information — a different kind of brain-correlate evidence from the 2015 structural finding above (connectivity, not grey-matter volume)."
  - year: "2021"
    title: "The first real conversation with a dreaming brain"
    who: "Karen Konkoly, Kristoffer Appel, Christopher Mallett et al. (four independent labs)"
    citation: "\"Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep,\" Current Biology, 31(7), 1417–1427"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Experimenters read simple maths problems aloud to people in confirmed REM sleep; dreamers answered correctly, from inside the dream, using pre-agreed eye or facial-muscle signals — roughly a quarter of 57 attempts succeeded. The first genuine two-way conversation with a sleeping, dreaming person, not just a one-way signal out."
    link: "https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2021/02/210218114018.htm"
    link_label: "Coverage of the study"
  - year: "2023"
    title: "A protocol that works roughly half the time — in the lab"
    who: "Michelle Carr, Karen Konkoly et al."
    citation: "\"Combining pre-sleep cognitive training and REM-sleep stimulation in a laboratory morning nap for lucid dream induction,\" Psychology of Consciousness: Theory, Research, and Practice, 10(4), 413"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Combined evening memory-training with a sound or light cue replayed during a REM-sleep nap, and hit roughly 50% success at inducing a signal-verified lucid dream — about three times the rate without cueing. This \"Targeted Lucidity Reactivation\" protocol is what OpenLucid's own long-term plan is built around."
    note: "Some reference lists cite this same underlying study as 2020 rather than 2023 — worth confirming the exact published-version year directly with the journal before citing it elsewhere."
  - year: "2024"
    title: "The same protocol, at home, unsupervised — and weaker"
    who: "Karen Konkoly et al."
    citation: "\"Provoking lucid dreams at home with sensory cues paired with pre-sleep cognitive training,\" Consciousness and Cognition, 125, 103759"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "Took the same cueing idea home, unsupervised, over a smartphone app. Once a proper control condition was added, the effect became weak and hard to attribute to the cueing itself — a concrete demonstration of exactly the gap OpenLucid's own research is trying to engineer around."
    link: "https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39278157/"
    link_label: "Read the paper"
  - year: "2025 (May)"
    title: "Evidence it's a genuinely separate state"
    who: "Çağatay Demirel et al."
    citation: "Journal of Neuroscience, 45(20), e2237242025"
    confidence: "established"
    summary: "The largest pooled EEG dataset of its kind, combined across multiple labs, found lucid REM sleep has its own distinct brain-connectivity signature (more alpha-band connectivity) — evidence lucid dreaming is a genuinely separate state of consciousness, not just ordinary REM sleep with extra awareness layered on top."
    link: "https://www.jneurosci.org/content/45/20/e2237242025"
    link_label: "Read the paper"
  - year: "2025"
    title: "Who tends to have them"
    who: "Arizona State University-led study"
    citation: "Published in the journal Dreaming"
    confidence: "reported"
    summary: "Linked personality traits — more openness and extraversion, less \"emotional stability\" — to how often someone reports lucid dreams."
    note: "Only verified here via the university's own press coverage, not the primary paper itself — treat this one as preliminary until the full citation is checked."
    link: "https://news.asu.edu/20251002-science-and-technology-dream-study-fatherdaughter-duo"
    link_label: "University press coverage"
