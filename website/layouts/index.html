One night, four steps.

A small crew works through the night so you don't have to.

Step 1

The headband listens

A consumer EEG headband picks up the tiny electrical whisper of your brain, all night long.

Step 2

It recognizes REM

Every 30 seconds, open-source sleep science guesses your stage: awake, deep sleep, or dreaming.

Step 3

A whisper

During stable REM it plays a soft sound you trained that evening to mean: am I dreaming?

Step 4

You signal back

Realize you're dreaming? Look left-right-left-right. Your eyes still move in REM, and the app writes it down.

Your brain data never leaves your house. No cloud. No account. No electricity into anyone's head.

Why Python on the server, and not Flutter end to end: sleep science runs on it. YASA, and the lab's own ezscore-f and eegFloss, are published in Python first — so the Diary Keeper can call them directly, no porting required, the day they're wired in.

Will it work?
Maybe.

Sound cues during REM sleep have helped some people become lucid in sleep labs. At home, with a consumer headband, the evidence is weaker and still being studied. OpenLucid doesn't promise you lucid dreams. It gives you an honest way to find out what works for you.
